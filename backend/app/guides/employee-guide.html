01

What this system is for

Trailer Check replaced the shared spreadsheet. Every trailer pickup is a ticket. A ticket is not finished when you save it — it's finished when QC approves it.

Your job is the first half of that: put a complete, truthful ticket in front of QC as fast as you can. The app decides on its own whether a ticket is complete. You never pick a status — you fill in fields, and the ticket moves itself.

The life of a ticket

YOU CREATE IT→ AWAITING DRIVER→ PENDING QC→ QC REVIEWS→ APPROVED   or   FLAGGED→ YOU FIX IT→ RESOLVED→ QC AGAIN
The one rule to remember

You never move a ticket forward by clicking a status button. You move it forward by filling in what's missing. The moment the last required field is satisfied, the ticket jumps to Pending QC by itself.

The statuses you'll see

StatusWhat it meansWhose move
Still SendingA draft you parked on purpose. Invisible to QC until you submit it.Yours
Awaiting DriverSaved, but something required is still missing — or you're waiting on a scale ticket.Yours
Pending QCComplete. It's in the QC queue now.QC's
FlaggedQC rejected it and told you why. It's on your Carryover board.Yours
ResolvedYou fixed it and resent it. Back in the QC queue.QC's
ApprovedPassed. +10 to your score.Nobody's — unless a scale ticket is still open.
Approved is not always "done"

If the pickup needed a scale ticket and you haven't checked Scale ticket received, the row stays on the Carryover board even after QC approves it. Approval ends the audit; it does not end the scale chase.

About the screenshots in this book

Truck numbers, driver names and carriers in these screenshots are practice data. Your screen will show real loads. If your computer is set to a light theme the colours will be lighter than shown here — the layout and every button are in exactly the same place.

02

Signing in

Your manager creates your account and hands you a username and password. There is no "sign up" — if you don't have an account, ask your manager, not the app.

1

Open the Trailer Check address your manager gave you. You land on the sign-in card.

2

Type your username and password and press Sign in (or just hit Enter). A wrong password shows a red bar and nothing else happens — no lockout, try again.

If the page hangs for a few seconds

The server sleeps when nobody's using it. The first sign-in of the morning can take 10–30 seconds and you may see a "waking up" message. Wait — don't keep clicking Sign in.

You stay signed in for 8 hours. After that you'll be bounced back here. Use Log out at the bottom of the sidebar if you share a computer with another shift.

Reading your screen

Everything lives in the left sidebar. It never moves. Learn these seven items and you know where everything is.

The badges

Red number on Carryover

Flagged tickets waiting on you. It pulses. Clear it before the end of your shift.

Amber number on Trailer Issues

Open trailer problems on the whole team's board. Calmer on purpose — it's a nudge, not an alarm.

Score pill, top right

Your running performance score. Starts at 100. +10 per approval, minus a penalty per flag.

Active Drafts

Anything you parked with Save Draft (Still Sending) sits here. Click the truck number to pick the form back up exactly where you left it; click the bin icon to throw the draft away if the load got cancelled.

CST vs Local

The CST / Local toggle changes only how times are printed on your screen. It does not change which shift a ticket belongs to — shifts are always Central Time, wherever you're sitting.

ShiftCentral Time window
First1:00 AM – 9:00 AM
Main9:00 AM – 5:00 PM
Third5:00 PM – 1:00 AM (crosses midnight)

Anything between midnight and 1:00 AM counts as the previous day's Third shift.

Pop-up messages

Small boxes appear at the bottom-right and disappear after about 12 seconds. Red = something needs you now (QC flagged your ticket). Amber = a reminder (items still missing, open trailer issues).

Full-width red bar across the top

That is the hazmat movement alert. UGL does not haul hazmat. If a load someone marked as hazmat starts rolling, every person logged in gets that bar, with the truck number and its speed. It stays up until someone dismisses it. Tell your manager immediately — don't just dismiss it.

04

Creating a pickup

This is the job. The form is long but it always runs in the same order, top to bottom: Truck → Trailer & Papers → PTI → Documents & Condition → Save.

Step 1 — Truck

1

Pick the Motor Carrier from the dropdown, then type the Truck Number. Get the carrier right first — the truck lookup only searches inside that carrier's fleet.

2

Wait about a second. Driver, Location, Model and Fuel % fill themselves in from the truck's telematics. Every one of those four fields is still yours to type over if the feed is wrong.

If nothing fills in

You'll get a small grey hint instead. That means the truck isn't in that carrier's fleet — usually the wrong carrier, or a typo in the number. It does not stop you saving; type the driver and location by hand and carry on.

Step 2 — "Still using trailer …?"

3

If this truck was here before, a box pops up the moment you finish typing the truck number, showing what it pulled last time.

  • Yes, same trailer — copies the trailer number and its PTI date into the form. Nothing else.
  • No, different trailer — closes the box and leaves the form alone.
Confirming does not verify anything

It fills in the trailer number and PTI date only. The papers and the PTI boxes stay unticked on purpose — you verify those fresh for this load, every time. Never treat the pop-up as "already checked".

Step 3 — Trailer & papers

4

Type the Trailer Number. It's optional on a normal pickup — but type it anyway. The trailer number is what makes the inspection and registration papers stick to that trailer forever.

As soon as a trailer number is present, a Saved papers box appears:

  • Save Inspection paper / Save Registration paper — upload a file.
  • Paste — paste a screenshot, a photo, or a copied link. If nothing happens when you click Paste, click it again and press Ctrl+V.
Why this saves you time

Papers are saved against the trailer, not this ticket. The next time that trailer shows up — next week, behind a different truck, on somebody else's shift — the papers are already attached. Upload once, never again.

5

LOT trailers

Flip LOT Trailer on for a trailer that lives on our own lot rather than arriving with the load. Two things change:

  • Trailer Number becomes required (it gets a red star).
  • A Last PTI Date field appears, with a warning if that date is stale.
The 7-day LOT rule

A LOT trailer can go to QC without a fresh PTI only if its last PTI is less than 7 days old. At 7 days or more you must tick the master PTI box. The form tells you which case you're in, in plain words, right under the date.

Step 4 — PTI verification

This panel has two separate things in it, and mixing them up is the single most common mistake new team members make.

The master box (red outline)

One checkbox. This alone is what marks PTI as done and what QC's gate reads.

The item list below it

25 lines. A log of what you saw in the video. It never blocks anything.

6

Watch the driver's PTI video. Tick the master box. Then use Select All (top right) or tick individual rows to record what you actually saw.

The green line at the bottom always tells you where you stand: PTI status: VERIFIED (master checkbox) · video log: 25 item(s) noted.

If the PTI hasn't come in yet

Leave the master box empty and an amber strip appears with Driver called and Informed carrier dispatch. Tick those to record that you're chasing it. They don't unblock anything — they're your proof that you did the follow-up. The strip disappears the moment you tick the master box.

7

Chassis loads

Turn on Is this a Chassis? and two extra rows appear at the bottom of the list: locks horizontal, and zip ties on locks. Only turn it on for an actual chassis.

Step 5 — Documents & condition

8

Work across the panel: hazmat, KPRA group, the six checkboxes, weight, condition.

The line above the checkboxes says it: “Inspection paper or sticker — one of the two is enough.” Ticking both is fine, ticking neither is what holds the ticket back.

Step 6 — Needs scale & the balancer

9

Tick Needs scale if this load has to cross a scale. The app warns you straight away: “Ticket will be saved to Carryover until the scale ticket arrives.” A Scale ticket received box appears next to it — leave it empty until the ticket is actually in your hand.

Ticking Needs scale also opens the DOT Predictive Balancer.

  1. Type the three scale weights — Steer (limit 12,000), Drive (34,000), Trailer (34,000).
  2. Anything over its limit turns red and the header flips from LEGAL TO ROLL to OVERWEIGHT.
  3. Read the Action Plan at the bottom: which way to slide, how many holes, and a safe maximum fuel level.
  4. Drag the 5th-wheel and tandem sliders to test a move before you tell the driver to make it.
The balancer is a scratch pad

Nothing you type into it is saved or sent anywhere. It's a calculator for you, on the spot. Use it freely; press Reset data when you're done.

Step 7 — Save

10

Two buttons at the bottom:

Create Ticket

Files it into the real workflow. It becomes Pending QC if everything's satisfied, otherwise Awaiting Driver. The form then clears for your next truck.

Save Draft (Still Sending)

Parks it. QC can't see it, it won't show on Carryover, and it won't appear in shift notes. The form clears so you can start another truck. Pick it back up from Active Drafts.

The green banner tells you which of the two happened. If it says the ticket went to Carryover instead, something on the list in chapter 6 is still missing.

Juggling several trucks at once

This is what Save Draft is for. Park truck A, start truck B, park B, come back to A from the Active Drafts panel in the sidebar. Nothing you parked is ever counted against you — drafts are invisible to QC and to the leaderboard until you submit them.

Don't lose a half-typed form

If you click away with unsaved changes, this appears. Save as Draft keeps your work. Leave (Discard Changes) throws it away and cannot be undone.

05

A pickup, click by click

The last chapter explained every field on the form. This one does one pickup from the first click to the green banner, in the exact order you would do it with a driver waiting. Follow it once with a real truck in front of you and you won't need it again.

The pickup we're doing

Driver at the gate. Truck 3318, carrier AL Amin, already hooked to trailer TRL-4412. Registration and inspection paper in your hand, BOL in the cab, PTI video just came through on your phone, and the load isn't crossing a scale. That's the ordinary case — the awkward versions are at the end of this chapter.

Open the form

1

Click New Pickup in the sidebar. You get an empty form with the Truck panel at the top. Nothing is saved and nothing is sent anywhere until you press a button at the bottom, so you can look around freely.

Carrier before truck

2

Open the Motor Carrier dropdown and pick AL Amin. Do this before you type the truck number — the lookup only searches inside the carrier you picked, so a truck typed under the wrong carrier simply won't be found.

3

Type 3318 into Truck Number and take your hands off the keyboard for about a second. Four fields fill themselves in: Driver James Carter, Location Chicago, IL — Yard 4, Model 2021 Volvo VNL 860, Fuel 63.

Read them. If the driver's name isn't the person standing in front of you, type over it — all four fields are yours to correct, and your correction is what QC sees.

Nothing filled in?

You get a small grey hint instead of the four fields. Nine times out of ten that's the wrong carrier or a typo in the number. Fix it if you can; if the truck is genuinely new, type the driver and location by hand and carry on. It does not block anything.

The pop-up about last time

4

The moment you finish the truck number, a box appears: Still using trailer TRL-4412? It is showing you what this truck pulled on its last ticket — the trailer number, the last PTI date (09/13/2026), which papers were verified then, and whether any trailer docs are on file.

The driver is on the same trailer, so click Yes, same trailer. That types TRL-4412 and the PTI date into the form for you.

It filled in two fields. It verified nothing.

Every checkbox is still empty after you click Yes, and that is deliberate. The papers and the PTI you are about to tick are for this load, today. Last week's ticket proves nothing about the trailer in front of you.

Trailer and papers

5

Look at the trailer's own plate and check it against the Trailer Number box. If the pop-up never appeared — new truck, or a different trailer this time — type the number in yourself. It is technically optional, and you should type it every single time anyway. The next step is why.

6

With a trailer number present, the Saved papers box appears. The pop-up told us Trailer Docs: None on file, so nobody has ever uploaded this trailer's papers. Do it now: Save Inspection paper and Save Registration paper, either by choosing a file or by hitting Paste with a photo on your clipboard.

A minute you spend once

Those files are filed against TRL-4412, not against this ticket. When that trailer rolls back in next month behind a different truck on somebody else's shift, the papers are already there. If the box already shows files, you have nothing to do here.

If the Paste button seems to do nothing, click it a second time and press Ctrl+V — the browser wants the keystroke before it hands over the clipboard.

7

LOT Trailer stays off. This trailer arrived with the load. You only flip it on for a trailer that was already sitting on our lot, and flipping it on makes the trailer number required and brings the 7-day PTI rule into play.

PTI — the step people get wrong

8

Watch the driver's video properly, then tick the master PTI checkbox — the one in the red outline at the top of the panel. That single box is what marks PTI done, and it is the only part of this panel QC's gate reads.

Now record what you saw: Select All at the top right if the video covered everything, or tick rows one at a time if it didn't. The bottom of the panel writes you a receipt — PTI status: VERIFIED (master checkbox) · video log: 25 item(s) noted. Read that line before you move on.

25 ticks and no master box means PTI is not verified

Going down all 25 rows and leaving the red-outlined box empty is the most common mistake on this form. The list is a log. The master box is the answer. Tick the box.

Is this a Chassis? stays off — a chassis adds two more rows about the locks, and this is a dry van.

Documents & condition

9

Work down the panel. Hazmat load off — it isn't hazmat, and turning it on puts a live GPS watch on the truck and alerts the whole team. Pick the KPRA destination group that matches where the load is going; ours is the 41ft group, and that is the kingpin limit you remind the driver about.

Then the checkboxes, with the papers in your hand as you go:

  • Registration verified — you're holding it. Tick.
  • Inspection paper verified — holding that too. Tick. Sticker verified can stay empty: one of the two is enough.
  • BOL present — it's in the cab. Tick.
  • ELD mentioned and Checklist sent — tick each one once you've actually done it. Neither blocks the ticket, and QC will flag you for leaving them blank anyway.
10

Weight is free text — type 41,250 lbs, or CRVR, or pending scale. Write what you actually know, not a guess. Set Trailer Condition to Good; anything other than Good needs a line in Condition Notes saying what is wrong with it.

11

Needs scale stays off for this load. If it did have to cross a scale you would tick it, the app would warn you that the ticket is parked on Carryover until the scale ticket arrives, and the DOT Predictive Balancer would open underneath for the three axle weights.

Check before you click

12

Five seconds, top to bottom, every time. These five are the only things that decide whether this ticket goes to QC or lands on your own Carryover board:

  1. Registration verified — ticked?
  2. Inspection paper or sticker — one of them ticked?
  3. BOL present — ticked?
  4. Needs scale off, or the scale ticket already in hand?
  5. Master PTI box ticked?

Save it

13

Click Create Ticket. A green banner comes back and the form clears itself for your next truck.

The banner is the whole answer. Complete, sent to QC review means you're done: the ticket is Pending QC and it's QC's move. If it says the ticket went to Carryover instead, one of the five checks above isn't satisfied — chapter 6 is the full list.

14

Open My Pickups if you want to see it with your own eyes: truck 3318 sits at the top of the list with a Pending QC badge. That is the pickup finished from your side.

The same pickup when it isn't tidy

Three things go sideways often enough to be worth knowing by heart. None of them is a reason to stop.

The PTI video hasn't arrived

Leave the master box empty. An amber strip appears — tick Driver called and Informed carrier dispatch as you do them. Click Create Ticket anyway: it saves as Awaiting Driver and waits for you on Carryover. Those two ticks are your proof that you chased it.

The phone rings halfway through

Save Draft (Still Sending). QC never sees a draft and it doesn't touch your score. Pick it back up from Active Drafts in the sidebar when you're free. If you click away without saving, the Unsaved changes box offers you the same thing — take it.

It's one of our own lot trailers

Flip LOT Trailer on. The trailer number becomes required and a Last PTI Date appears. Under 7 days old and you're clear; 7 days or more and the line turns amber — get a fresh PTI and tick the master box.

The whole chapter in one breath

Carrier, truck, wait a second. Confirm the trailer. Papers on file. Watch the video, tick the master box. Registration, inspection, BOL. Weight and condition. Read the green banner.

06

The five rules that send it to QC

A ticket becomes Pending QC the instant all five of these are true — and not one second before. Nothing else on the form matters to the gate.

  1. Registration verified is ticked.
  2. Inspection paper verified or Sticker verified is ticked. Either one.
  3. BOL present is ticked.
  4. If Needs scale is on, Scale ticket received is also ticked.
  5. PTI passes: the master PTI box is ticked — or it's a LOT trailer whose last PTI is under 7 days old.
What is NOT on this list

ELD mentioned, Checklist sent, Weight, KPRA group, Trailer Condition and the 25-item PTI log do not hold a ticket back. Fill them in anyway — QC can and will flag you for leaving them blank, which costs you score. They just aren't what the gate reads.

The gate is re-checked every single time you save anything, including a single tick on the Carryover board. That's why a checkbox there can make a row vanish: it just completed the gate and jumped into the QC queue.

07

Carryover & the scale chase

Carryover is your to-do list. It has two halves: cards at the top for anything QC flagged, and a table underneath for everything still waiting on a scale ticket.

The scale-chase table

A row appears here the moment you tick Needs scale, and it leaves the instant you tick Scale Tkt. That is the only way off this board. Its status — even Approved — makes no difference.

Reading the timers

TierWaitingWhat you seeDo this
Normalunder 2hPlain rowNothing yet.
Warning2h – 4hAmber row, pulsing warning circleChase the driver or the scale house.
Critical4h+Red row, pulsing red dot, sorted to the very topChase it now and put it in the shift notes.

The buttons on a row

Followed up (clock icon)

Next to the truck number. Press it when you've chased the driver again — it restarts the waiting timer and clears the overdue colour. The original request time stays on record permanently, so this is not a way to hide a delay.

Checkbox columns

Reg, Insp, Sticker, BOL, ELD, CklSt, PTI, Driver Called, Informed Carrier, Scale Tkt. Tick one and it saves immediately — no Save button, no modal.

Trailer # and Weight cells

Click the text, type, then press Enter or click away. It only saves if you actually changed it.

Dropped (box icon)

The truck dropped its trailer and there's nothing left to process. It freezes the ticket and takes it off every active board. It stays in the history.

Greyed-out rows

Rows created by another team member show with their checkboxes disabled. That's deliberate — you can see their work but you can't edit it. QC and managers can.

08

When QC flags you

A flag is not a telling-off. It's a list of exactly what to fix, with the QC's own words attached. Fix it, resend it, move on.

How you find out

  • A red pop-up at the bottom-right: “QC flagged your ticket for truck 1265 — see Action Required.”
  • The red number on the Carryover item in the sidebar goes up and pulses.

Reading the card

Each Action Required card shows the truck number, one red tag per problem (Missing sticker, Incorrect weight…), and a QC notes line with the reviewer's explanation. Underneath is the same row of checkboxes as Carryover, so you can fix most things without leaving the page.

1

Read the tags and the QC note. Do the real-world work — get the sticker photo, re-weigh, chase the paper.

2

Tick the boxes on the card that are now true, or press Open full form if you need the whole intake screen.

3

Press Fixed — resend to QC. The card disappears and you get a confirmation line.

Urgent flags

A card with a pulsing Urgent — anyone can fix badge is open to the whole floor, not just whoever created it. If you fix someone else's urgent flag you earn a +5 teamwork bonus. If you're free and one is sitting there, take it.

Mistake privacy

A normal flag is visible only to you, QC and managers. Nobody else on the floor sees it. Urgent flags are the deliberate exception, because they need the whole team.

When you genuinely can't fix it

1

Press Mark Unresolvable / Can't Fix on the card.

2

Write what actually happened — “Trailer left the yard before we could photograph the registration. Owner-operator unreachable until Monday.” Vague reasons come straight back to you.

3

Press Escalate to QC. It leaves your board and lands in QC's queue as an Exception Review. QC either force-approves it with your explanation permanently attached, or flags it again and it comes back to you.

Use this sparingly

This is for things that are physically impossible to fix — not for things that are inconvenient or that you'd rather do tomorrow. Every escalation is on permanent record with your name on it.

09

Shift notes & handover

The handover writes most of itself. Your job at the end of a shift is to read it, add anything the app can't know, and publish.

1

Open Notes. The lines with a sparkle icon were written automatically from your own open tickets — one line per truck, listing exactly what's missing. Nobody types these.

Each has an Edit Pickup button. If you can close the gap right now, do it — the line disappears by itself.

2

Add anything the app can't know in the text box: “Scale house at Elwood closes at 22:00 tonight.”

One line = one note

Every non-empty line in that box becomes its own separate note. Paste a five-line list and you get five notes the next shift can tick off one at a time — not one paragraph they have to read whole.

3

Press Publish Shift Handover. Everything moves to the Global Shift Notes — Team Inbox, where the next shift sees it.

4

Coming on shift, read the Team Inbox first. Press Done on anything you've dealt with so the board stays honest.

Publish once

A line that's already on the team board won't be published twice. If you delete or reword a published note, it is not silently re-created — so don't delete something you still need.

10

Trailer issues

When QC finds something physically wrong with a trailer that isn't a team member's mistake, they file a trailer issue instead of flagging anyone.

This is not a flag

A trailer issue costs nobody any points, doesn't block an approval and doesn't appear on anyone's record. It's a broadcast to the whole team that a piece of equipment needs attention.

Anyone can press Resolved once it's genuinely handled — the trailer went to the shop, or the part was replaced. While any issue stays open the amber badge sits on the sidebar and the app will nudge the floor about it roughly every two hours.

11

Finding old pickups

My Pickups — your own work

  • Active Pickups — anything still moving.
  • All My Pickups — everything, including dropped and approved.
  • Search by truck or trailer number, filter by status or by day.

You can edit your own ticket here even after it's been approved — the pencil icon. This is the one place a team member can touch a finished ticket, so use it for genuine corrections only.

All Pickups — the whole floor

Use this before you start a pickup to check nobody has already done that truck today. Two sections: Active Pickups (everything still moving, all employees) and the Full Sheet (every ticket ever). A dash in a tick column means "not applicable"; n/a in the SCALE column means the load never needed one.

12

Your score & the leaderboard

There are two numbers and they mean different things. Don't confuse them.

1. Your score pill (top right of every page)

A running tally. Everyone starts at 100 and it never drops below 0.

EventChange
Your ticket gets approved+10
You fix an urgent flag somebody else created+5
Flagged — Missed PTI−20
Flagged — Missing BOL−15
Flagged — Missing Registration−15
Flagged — Missing Inspection−15
Flagged — PTI video missing the light test−15
Flagged — Incorrect Weight−10
Flagged — Missing Sticker−10
Flagged — Didn't remind the driver about KPRA law−10
Flagged — Didn't text in the group−1 to −10 (QC sets the severity)
Flagged — Other−5
One flag can carry several categories

QC can tick more than one box on a single flag, and each one costs you separately. A flag for "missing sticker + incorrect weight" is −20, not −10.

2. The leaderboard score

This is worked out fresh from your record, not accumulated:

Accuracy — 70% of it

The share of your tickets that were never flagged. One flag marks that ticket forever, even after you fix it.

Efficiency — 30% of it

How long from creating a ticket to it reaching QC. Under 15 minutes on average scores 100; you lose 10 points for every extra minute.

Volume multiplier

Scales with how many tickets you've done, reaching full weight at 50. One flawless pickup scores about 17, not 100 — consistency beats a lucky single.

How to score well

Finish tickets promptly and don't get flagged. Both halves reward the same habit: do the checks properly the first time, and don't leave a half-finished ticket sitting for an hour before you complete it.

Quick reference

Your shift, start to finish

  1. Sign in. Read the Notes → Team Inbox the previous shift left you.
  2. Check Carryover: clear the red badge (flags) and chase anything red or amber in the scale table.
  3. Work pickups in New Pickup. Park what you can't finish with Save Draft.
  4. Re-check your drafts and Carryover once an hour — the app will nudge you.
  5. Before you leave: open Notes, read the auto-written lines, add anything else, Publish Shift Handover.

Things that are easy to get wrong

  • The master PTI box is what counts. Ticking all 25 items without the master box means PTI is not verified.
  • Inspection or sticker — one is enough. Neither is not.
  • Approved ≠ off Carryover if a scale ticket is still outstanding.
  • Confirming “same trailer” fills in the number and date. It does not verify papers or PTI.
  • Drafts are invisible to QC. A parked draft is not a submitted ticket.
  • Hazmat is not a routine checkbox. Turning it on alerts the entire company.
