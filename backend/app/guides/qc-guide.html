01

What QC is for

Nothing leaves this system without a human having looked at it. That human is you. A team member's ticket is only "done" once you say so.

TEAM MEMBER FILLS IT IN→ PENDING QC→ YOU REVIEW IT→ APPROVED  or  FLAGGED→ THEY FIX IT→ RESOLVED→ YOU AGAIN

What lands in your queue

StatusHow it got hereWhat's different
Pending QCThe team member satisfied all five readiness rules.A first look.
ResolvedYou (or another QC) flagged it, the team member fixed it and resent.The card carries a purple “Previously flagged for — verify these fixes” panel with the old flag and its note. Check that first.
Exception ReviewThe team member hit a wall and escalated instead of fixing.Red border, their written reason at the top, and Approve is relabelled Force Approve.

The five readiness rules

These are what got the ticket to you. They are not your audit standard — they're the floor, not the bar. Knowing them tells you what the system already guaranteed, so you can spend your attention on what it can't check.

  1. Registration verified.
  2. Inspection paper verified or Sticker verified — either one.
  3. BOL present.
  4. If the load needs a scale, the scale ticket has been received.
  5. PTI passes — the master PTI box is ticked, or it's a LOT trailer whose last PTI is under 7 days old.
What the system cannot check

That the sticker photo is legible. That the weight matches what the driver actually texted. That the PTI video really showed the light test. That the KPRA group matches the destination. Those are the things you are here for.

About the screenshots in this book

Truck numbers, carriers and names are practice data. If your computer uses a light theme the colours will be lighter than shown — every control is in the same place.

02

Your screen

Sign in with the username and password your manager issued. You get every page the rest of the team gets, plus two of your own.

QC Review

Your queue. Every ticket waiting on a verdict.

My Audits

Everything you've approved or flagged, dated by when you acted.

New Pickup & Carryover

You create pickups too. Everything in the Team Handbook applies to you as well.

Trailer Issues

The equipment problems you file land here for the whole floor.

Notifications you'll get

  • Red pop-up: a team member resent a flagged ticket — “jmartinez resent truck 2277 B after fixes — ready to verify.” It's back in your queue.
  • Amber pop-up: a reminder about open trailer issues or missing items on your own pickups.
  • Full-width red bar: a hazmat-marked truck is moving. Everyone logged in sees it. Escalate to a manager immediately.

The CST / Local toggle at the bottom of the sidebar changes only how times are printed. Shifts are always Central Time: First 1:00–9:00 AM, Main 9:00 AM–5:00 PM, Third 5:00 PM–1:00 AM.

03

Working the queue

The control bar

ControlWhat it does
SearchTruck number, trailer number or driver.
All shiftsNarrow to First, Main or Third — Central Time, always.
All employeesReview one team member's work in a run. Useful when you're coaching someone.
All statusesSeparate the first looks from the re-checks.
Oldest firstLeave it here. Oldest first is what keeps the floor's turnaround honest — and your own efficiency score depends on it.
Include awaiting scale ticketOff by default. Turn it on to pre-read tickets that are still waiting on a scale ticket. You can't usefully approve them yet, but you can spot problems early.

The order to work in

  1. Exception Reviews first — red border. Somebody is blocked and waiting on your decision.
  2. Resolved next — a team member has already done the rework. Don't leave them hanging.
  3. Pending QC, oldest first — the default order. Just work down it.
Turnaround is measured

Your leaderboard efficiency is the average time from a ticket entering the queue to your verdict. Under 15 minutes scores full marks; every extra minute costs 10 points. Cherry-picking easy cards off the bottom of the list hurts you twice — it ages the hard ones and drags your average.

04

Anatomy of a review card

Every card is laid out identically. Learn it once and you can read one in about ten seconds.

Top block — identity and telematics

FieldWhere it comes fromWhat to watch for
Truck number + status badgeTyped by the team member.Badges beside it: Hazmat and LOT Trailer.
CreatedWhen the ticket was opened.A long gap between this and now means a slow ticket.
MCSelected by the team member.Wrong carrier is why telematics fields come back blank.
Trailer #Typed. You can edit this inline.A magnifier next to the label opens Trailer Lookup — chapter 5.
Created byThe team member.If it's you, see chapter 11.
Driver / Location / ModelAuto-filled from telematics, editable by the team member.A dash means the truck wasn't found — usually the wrong MC.
FuelCaptured once, at intake.It does not update by itself. The circular arrow beside it pulls the live reading — use it before you judge anything fuel-related.
WeightFree text. You can edit this inline.pending scale is normal on a scale load.
ConditionGood / Fair / Damaged.Anything but Good should have notes — and may deserve a trailer issue.

Middle block — the history trio

Three server-computed fields so you're never auditing a returning trailer blind:

Last PTI Date

The most recent known PTI for this trailer or truck, from any ticket. This is the field the 7-day LOT rule reads.

Last QC Approved

The last time this trailer passed QC on a different ticket.

Last Hauled By

The most recent other truck that pulled this trailer, and when. Trailer-scoped, not truck-scoped.

Evidence block

Saved trailer papers

Papers are saved against the trailer and follow it between pickups. The box always tells you which state it's in and never sits silently blank:

  • No trailer linked — no trailer number on the ticket.
  • Checking… — still loading.
  • No papers on file yet for trailer X — genuinely none saved.
  • Couldn't load — a network problem, not an empty record. Refresh before you judge.
  • Links — open them and actually look at them.

Status chips

One row of chips summarising the checklist: green OK, red Missing, for Registration, Inspection, Sticker, BOL, PTI, ELD and Checklist, plus an amber KPRA chip naming the destination group when one was chosen.

A red chip is not automatically a flag

Sticker: Missing is fine when Inspection: OK — only one of the two is required. ELD: Missing and Checklist: Missing don't block the gate at all, which is exactly why they end up in front of you: the system let them through, so it's your call whether they matter on this load.

PTI video log

The log never gates anything

The master PTI checkbox is what marks PTI as done. The 25-item log is context. A log reading 0/25 items noted alongside PTI: OK is a legitimate state — but it's also worth asking about, and that's a judgement call, not a rule the system enforces.

05

Trailer history & lookup

A trailer that's new to this truck may not be new to the fleet. Before you flag a stale or missing PTI, check whether another truck already did one.

1

Click the small magnifier next to the Trailer # label. A Trailer Lookup popover opens, pre-filled with the trailer currently on the ticket.

2

You can type any trailer number in the box — useful when you suspect a typo and want to see what the corrected number actually belongs to. Press Check.

3

Read the two results:

  • Registered Last PTI — the date stored against that trailer.
  • Last hauled by — the truck and date of its most recent other pickup.

Apply to Ticket is greyed out for you: “Manager privileges required to apply this correction.” If the registered date is genuinely wrong, get a manager — they can correct it in place from the card, and the correction is written against the trailer, so it shows up everywhere that trailer appears next.

Why this matters

Without this, a perfectly current trailer that arrived behind a different truck looks like a missing PTI, and you flag a team member who did nothing wrong. Check the history before you spend somebody's points.

06

The four verdicts

Every card ends in one of four actions. Picking the right one is most of this job. Work down this ladder and stop at the first that fits.

Approve

Everything checks out. The paperwork is there, legible, and consistent with the telematics and the weights. Tick the three confirmations and approve. The creator gets +10.

Fix it yourself

A small, objective typo you can see the right answer to — a mistyped trailer number, a weight that's obviously a slip. Correct it inline and approve. No flag, no penalty, no round trip.

Trailer issue

The trailer is broken, but nobody made a mistake. Cracked mudflap bracket, bent landing gear. File the issue and then still approve or flag the ticket on its own merits.

Flag

The team member missed something they should have caught, or you need real-world work done that only they can do. Pick the categories, write what you need, send it back.

The test that separates a fix from a flag

Ask: can I see the correct answer from here? If yes, fix it. If the correction needs somebody to go and look at a trailer, re-shoot a photo, re-weigh a load or post in the group — flag it. Never flag something you could have fixed in four seconds; never quietly "fix" something you're actually guessing at.

07

Fixing it yourself

Two fields on every card are live inputs, not labels: Trailer # and Weight.

1

Click the value. It's already an input — no edit button to find.

2

Type the correction.

3

Press Enter or click away. It saves immediately, and only if the value actually changed.

Correcting a trailer number is not a small act

The trailer record carries the saved papers. The system is built to rename the existing trailer in place so its papers follow — unless the number you typed already belongs to a different known trailer, in which case the ticket re-links to that trailer and its papers instead. That's the right outcome for a typo fix. It is the wrong outcome if you were guessing. Use Trailer Lookup first.

Refreshing fuel

Fuel % is captured once, when the pickup was created, and never updates on its own. The circular arrow next to it pulls the live reading from telematics. Use it before you judge anything that depends on fuel — and remember the team member's axle-balance plan may have assumed a full tank.

08

Approving

Approval is deliberately slower than one click. Three boxes, each a separate thing you are putting your name to.

1

Press Approve Ticket. A confirmation box opens with all three boxes empty and Confirm Approval greyed out.

2

Tick each one only when it is actually true:

ConfirmationWhat you are asserting
Data matches Samsara/TelematicsDriver, location and model on the card match the live feed — or the team member's override is right and you know why.
Weights are within legal boundsThe recorded weight is plausible and, on a scale load, the scale ticket backs it up. Steer 12,000 / drive 34,000 / trailer 34,000.
Documentation is visually confirmedYou opened the papers and looked at them. Not "the chip is green" — you looked.
3

Press Confirm Approval. The card leaves the queue and a green line confirms who got the credit.

The three boxes are the point

They exist to stop rubber-stamping. Ticking them without doing the checks is the one failure mode this whole system can't catch — every approval is permanently logged with your name, the time and the ticket.

Approval doesn't clear the scale chase

If the load needed a scale and the scale ticket hasn't been received, the row stays on the Carryover board after you approve it, marked Approved — scale pending. That's correct. Somebody still has to chase the ticket.

09

Flagging

A flag sends the ticket back to the team member's Carryover board with your reasons attached, and costs them score. Be specific and be fair.

Step 1 — Pick every category that applies

Tick all of them, not just the worst one. One flag can carry several categories, and each one is recorded and penalised separately.

CategoryUse it whenCosts them
Missed PTINo PTI was done, or it was marked done without one.−20
Missing BOLNo bill of lading, or it was ticked without one.−15
Missing RegistrationNo registration on file or in hand.−15
Missing InspectionNo inspection paper and no sticker.−15
PTI — video wasn't with the light testThe PTI video skipped the lights.−15
Incorrect weightThe recorded weight doesn't match the scale or what the driver reported.−10
Missing stickerSticker claimed but not visible or not legible.−10
Didn't remind the driver about KPRA lawDestination group needed a reminder and none was given.−10
Didn't text in the groupLoad moved with nothing posted in the group chat.−1 to −10 (you choose)
OtherAnything else. Notes become mandatory.−5

Step 2 — Two categories change the form

“Didn't text in the group”

A Communication failure severity slider appears, 1 to 10, defaulting to 5. The number you pick is the penalty. 1 for a late post, 8–10 for a load that left with nothing said at all.

“Other”

Notes stop being optional. Describe the problem or the form won't submit.

Step 3 — Write the note

The note is the only part of a flag the team member can act on. A category tells them what; the note tells them what to do next.

Good

“Sticker photo never came through, and nothing was posted in the group when this load left. Re-shoot the sticker and post it.”

Useless

“Incomplete.” / “Fix this.” / “See categories.”

Step 4 — Urgent, or not

What Urgent actually does

A normal flag is visible only to its creator, QC and managers — that's mistake privacy, and it's deliberate. Turning on Urgent throws that away: the ticket appears on every team member's Action Required board with a pulsing badge, and whoever fixes it earns a +5 teamwork bonus if they didn't create it. Use it when the load is moving and it can't wait for one person — not to make a point.

Step 5 — Attach proof

Upload file or paste a media URL and press Add URL. Images and videos are detected from the file extension. A screenshot of the illegible sticker ends the argument before it starts.

Step 6 — Submit

The card leaves your queue. The team member gets a red pop-up within about fifteen seconds and a red badge on their Carryover item. When they resend it, it comes back to you as Resolved, carrying a purple panel with your original flag and note so you can check exactly what you asked for.

10

Trailer issues

For a real problem with the equipment that isn't anybody's mistake. This is the tool that stops you having to choose between blaming someone and saying nothing.

1

Press Trailer Issue on the card. A box opens inside it.

2

Describe what a mechanic would need to know — “Rear passenger-side mudflap bracket is cracked — not the team member's miss, but shop needs to see it before the next load.” At least three characters, but write a real sentence.

3

Press Report Issue. It goes to the whole team's Trailer Issues board and nudges the floor about every two hours until someone marks it resolved.

It isn't an audit verdict

Because it's a report and not a judgement, a trailer issue carries no score penalty, doesn't block approval, and is available even on a pickup you created. Filing one and then approving the same ticket is a completely normal outcome.

Issue or flag?

The trailer is broken → trailer issue. The team member didn't notice the trailer was broken, or logged it as Good → that's a flag, and you can file the issue as well. Both, when both are true.

11

Exceptions & conflicts

Exception Review — when a team member can't fix it

A flagged ticket the team member genuinely can't resolve comes back to you as an Exception Review: red border, their mandatory written reason at the top, the flag history below, and Approve relabelled Force Approve.

Force Approve

You accept the exception. The ticket closes with their explanation permanently on record. Use when the reason is genuine and the load can't wait.

Flag it again

You reject the escalation. It clears the exception and restarts the normal fix loop. Use when the reason is vague, or when it plainly could be fixed.

Read the reason, not just the fact of it

“Trailer left the yard before we could photograph the registration; owner-operator unreachable until Monday” is a real constraint. “Couldn't get it” is not. Force Approve puts your name on accepting that explanation, so hold it to the standard you'd want applied to your own.

Your own pickups — conflict of interest

QC creates pickups too. You can never approve or flag a ticket you created. Approve and Flag are replaced by a plain notice.

This is enforced on the server, not just hidden

There is no workaround, no admin toggle, and no way to do it from another page. Another QC user or a manager has to audit it. Managers are exempt from this rule; you are not. Trailer Issue stays available, because a report about broken equipment isn't a verdict on yourself.

12

My Audits & your score

Everything you decide is on permanent record, and you can read it back at any time.

The Current state column is the useful one. A flag you raised that's still sitting at Flagged days later is a ticket nobody has picked up — chase it, or raise it in the handover.

How QC is scored on the leaderboard

Accuracy — 70%

Fixed at 100 for QC. There is no counter-signal in the system that could fairly mark a reviewer down, so it doesn't pretend to have one.

Efficiency — 30%

Average time from a ticket entering the queue to your verdict. Under 15 minutes scores 100; every extra minute costs 10 points.

Volume multiplier

Approvals plus flags. Reaches full weight at 50 actions, so a handful of reviews can't outrank a full shift's work.

Your score does not reward approving

Approvals and flags count identically toward volume, and accuracy is fixed. There is no mathematical incentive to wave things through — only turnaround is measured. Take the time a card needs.

You also have a score pill of your own, top right, because you create pickups too. That one follows the same rules as everybody else: +10 when a ticket you created is approved, and penalties if one gets flagged.

13

Quick reference

Reviewing one card

  1. Read the status badge. Resolved → read the purple “previously flagged” panel first and check only that the fixes landed.
  2. Check the history trio — Last PTI, Last QC Approved, Last Hauled By. Open Trailer Lookup if the PTI looks stale.
  3. Open the saved papers and actually look at them.
  4. Read the status chips. Remember: inspection or sticker; ELD and Checklist never gated.
  5. Expand the PTI video log if the load or the trailer gives you any reason to.
  6. Refresh fuel if anything depends on it.
  7. Fix what you can see the answer to. File a trailer issue if equipment is broken.
  8. Approve (three boxes) or Flag (every category, a real note, proof).

Rules that never bend

  • You can never approve or flag a pickup you created.
  • All three confirmation boxes, every approval, with the checks actually done.
  • Every flag gets a note that says what to do next.
  • Trailer issues cost nobody any points — use them freely, they're the right tool for broken equipment.
  • Urgent removes mistake privacy from the whole floor. Mean it when you use it.
  • Apply to Ticket in Trailer Lookup is manager-only. Get a manager; don't work around it.
